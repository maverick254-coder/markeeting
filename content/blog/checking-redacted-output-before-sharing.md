Most redaction mistakes are not made while marking. They are discovered, or missed, after export.

A page can look correct in the editor and still produce an output that needs another look. A mark might sit slightly off the text it was meant to cover. A page might have been skipped. A recording might carry a sensitive phrase a second earlier than the range that was drawn.

That is why the export deserves its own review step. Here is a checklist reviewers can work through before a file leaves the team.

## 1. Open the output, not the project

Review the file that will actually be shared. The project view shows your decisions; the exported copy shows their result. Open it the way the recipient will, in an ordinary viewer or player.

RedactPlus writes a new redacted copy and leaves the original untouched, so the source remains available if anything needs to change.

## 2. Check completeness page by page

Scroll every page, including the ones you expect to be clean. Pay particular attention to:

- headers, footers and page margins
- tables, signatures and stamps
- scanned pages, where text was recognised rather than read directly
- attachments or appended pages added late in the process

If a page was converted from another format before review, check that the layout came through as expected.

## 3. Confirm that what should remain is still readable

Over-redaction is also a failure. A disclosure that hides the context it was meant to share may not serve its purpose.

Read the surrounding sentences for each mark. Make sure dates, headings and non-sensitive references that the recipient needs are still visible.

## 4. Check reasons and consistency

When the same name or reference appears in several places, it should be treated the same way each time. Where your process records a reason for each redaction, skim the reasons for anything that looks out of place.

A report of the reviewed redaction actions helps here. It gives a second reviewer something concrete to check against.

## 5. For audio, listen at the edges

In a recording, the risky moments are the boundaries of each range. Play a few seconds before and after every redacted section. Listen for words that start just before a range begins or finish just after it ends.

## 6. Decide, then record the decision

Once the output has been checked, note who reviewed it and when, following your organization's own process.

## Why this matters

Detection tools can surface candidates, but no tool can guarantee that every sensitive item has been found. A short, consistent output check is what turns a set of marks into a file a team can stand behind.
