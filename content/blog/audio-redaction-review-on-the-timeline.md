Recorded interviews, support calls, hearings and research sessions often contain the same sensitive details as documents: names, addresses, account numbers and health information.

The difference is that nobody can glance at a recording. A ten-page document can be skimmed in minutes. A one-hour interview takes an hour to hear in full.

That makes audio redaction a different kind of review.

## A redaction is a time range

On a page, a redaction covers an area. In a recording, it covers a span of time: a start point and an end point.

Getting those points right matters. Start too late and the first syllable of a name is still audible. End too early and the last digits of a number come through. Cut too wide and the listener loses context that was meant to be shared.

RedactPlus lets reviewers draw, adjust and review redaction ranges directly on an audio timeline, alongside the waveform.

## Transcripts help reviewers navigate

A transcript turns a recording into something that can be read and searched. Where a transcript is available, reviewers can use it as context while they work: find the moment a name is spoken, then check that point on the timeline.

A transcript is a guide, not the final answer. Speech recognition can mishear names, accents and overlapping voices. The recording itself is what gets exported, so the timeline is where the decision is confirmed.

## Choosing how a redaction sounds

What the listener hears in place of the removed audio is a deliberate choice. Common options include:

- **Mute or silence**, which removes the sound completely
- **A tone**, which makes it clear that something was removed
- **A noise mask**, which covers speech less abruptly

RedactPlus supports effects including mute, tone, silence and noise masks, depending on the configured media tooling. Effects can be previewed before export.

## Listen before you share

The exported recording deserves the same scrutiny as a redacted PDF. Before it is shared:

1. Play a few seconds either side of every redacted range.
2. Confirm that the chosen effect covers the full spoken item.
3. Check that the remaining content still makes sense to the intended listener.

Audio redaction rewards patience. Precise ranges, careful listening and a final check of the rendered file are what make a recording ready to share.
